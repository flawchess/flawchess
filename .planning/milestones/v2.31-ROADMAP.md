# v2.31 Facepalm Refits & Move Quality — Phase Detail

**Shipped:** 2026-11-23 (deployed to production, releases #492–#506)
**Phases:** 257–262
**Full roadmap context:** [../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md)

Archived phase detail, moved out of the active roadmap at milestone close. The working
directories for these phases live in [v2.31-phases/](v2.31-phases/).

Six phases that take the facepalm score from a blunder-check selector to a first-class part of the analysis. Phase 257 adds a facepalm rate to Library Stats (SEED-261114-iuv), Phase 258 bases the snapshot charts on a recent-games sample (SEED-261121-3ou), Phase 259 refits the score on what a human reads from the lines in three releases (SEED-261121-tau), Phase 260 makes facepalm its own move quality with a ??? marker (SEED-261122-j9p), Phase 261 serves about 20% of blunder-check windows from an exploration arm (SEED-261121-t50), and Phase 262 counts promotions as material and scores each blunder on its key move (SEED-261122-s0j).

### Phase 257: Facepalm Rate in the Library Stats (SEED-261114-iuv)

**Goal**: Add facepalm statistics to the library stats, limited to the part that needs no benchmark: the
facepalm share of the player's blunders over time, a "you vs your opponents" facepalm comparison (total and per
type), and the facepalm chip tinted by type. Decisions in the seed ("Current scope", 2026-11-20, which wins over
the older sections).

- **Stats cut:** a new constant at 0.7 (the label meaning), separate from the drill's `FACEPALM_SCORE_MIN`
  (0.5). Lambda is `FACEPALM_LIBRARY_LAMBDA`.
- **Type per blunder** on the model's raw material inputs: allowed loss when `loss > 0 and loss >= missed`,
  missed win otherwise (ties count as allowed loss, rows without material as missed win). Returned by the
  library API next to the score.
- **Chip colour by type:** `TAC_MISSED` (teal) for missed win, `TAC_ALLOWED` (crimson) for allowed loss.
- **Timeline:** facepalm blunders / scored blunders, pooled over the rolling window, not split by type. Its own
  small chart below the Flaws Timeline (the timeline axis is flaws per 100 moves, a share cannot sit on it), with
  the same x-axis, rolling window and per-time-control series. Source is `game_flaws`; unscored blunders are left
  out of both counts. A rolling mean of the score is the fallback if the share is too jumpy on real accounts.
- **You vs your opponents:** facepalm blunders per game, paired per game; a total row and one row per type. The
  per-type rows are the chance blindness (missed win) and threat blindness (allowed loss) readings.
- **No type-share statistic** (design note, accepted 2026-11-20): the model weights lost material about three
  times as much as a missed gain, so the types are never compared with each other, only with the same type of
  the opponents. Copy states the comparison and does not diagnose; the score is a ranking, not a probability.
- **Opponent scores stay out of drill selection and the library tag filters** (user-only, `player_only_gate`).
- **Open for discuss-phase:** the minimum number of scored blunders per timeline point; where the comparison
  sits in the stats table.
- **Out of scope** (SEED-261120-bzq): benchmark zones, the within-player bias against a benchmark, the
  type-by-clock matrix, the training weighting.

**Depends on**: Phase 256 (shipped model and constants), Phase 253 (score chip and library payload) and the
opponent blunder scoring on prod (seed Part A, done: Maia columns 99.2%, line facts 93.9%, the same coverage as
user blunders). Does not wait for the Phase 252 repair: the gap in line facts is the same on both sides, so it
does not skew the paired comparison.

### Phase 258: Library Stats Recent Sample for Snapshot Charts (SEED-261121-3ou)

**Goal**: Make the Library Stats snapshot charts describe how the player plays now, while the timelines keep
the whole career. Today the page defaults to all time, so the "you vs your opponents" flaw, facepalm and tactic
comparisons lump a career into one average and are graded against zones built on recent games. Decisions in the
seed ("Decided Shape", 2026-11-21).

- **Split by chart type, no filter-default changes.** The shared filter store keeps its defaults, so the Library
  Games and Flaws tabs are untouched.
- **Timelines keep full history.** Sidebar filters, including an explicit recency choice, still crop them.
- **Snapshot charts always use a recent sample:** `/library/flaw-comparison`, `/library/facepalm-comparison` and
  `/library/tactic-comparison` are computed on the most recent 1000 games per time control within the last 36
  months (`RECENT_GAMES_PER_TC_CAP`, `RECENCY_WINDOW_MONTHS`), taken from the games the sidebar filters leave.
- **An explicit recency filter replaces the 36-month bound;** the 1000-game cap stays.
- **The cap counts all filtered games, then the analyzed subset is shown,** so the window matches Endgames and
  stays time-defined.
- **One shared windowed game set** for the counts and all three comparisons, so the basis line and the tiles
  always agree.
- **Basis line under the snapshot section header,** for example "Based on your most recent 1000 blitz games
  (since Mar 2025), 640 analyzed". The API returns the window start and the sample sizes.
- **No "Recent / All games" switch:** the lifetime snapshot is deliberately unreachable.
- **No default opponent strength of ±100:** the flaw zones are already equal-footing calibrated and the user's
  value stays unfiltered; the filter remains available.
- **Performance check on a large account:** the ranked "recent 1000 per time control" subquery is a new query
  shape on a page where the facepalm comparison was already split out for speed.
- **Accepted:** the last timeline point (trailing 100 games) and the snapshot tile (up to 1000 games) show
  different numbers.
- **Open for discuss-phase:** whether the cap applies before or after the "played as" colour filter (same period
  for both colours, or the most recent 1000 games of that colour).
- **Out of scope:** restoring full-history timelines on Endgames Stats with the same split (a separate
  decision); any change to the Endgames ranked sample.

**Depends on**: Phase 257 (the facepalm comparison endpoint and the current Library Stats layout).

### Phase 259: Facepalm Score Refit on First Principles (SEED-261121-tau)

**Goal**: Make the facepalm score read what a human would read from the lines, then refit it. Two changes share
one refit. Found on prod game 2883555 ply 47: an even four-capture trade in the best line scored as a missed
queen, and the model has no input for how findable the punishing move is. Details and the term breakdown in the
seed.

- **Best-reply probability:** a stored `maia_best_reply_prob` on `game_flaws`, Maia's probability at the
  blunderer's pinned Elo of the engine's best reply to the blunder. Allowed losses then have the same pair of
  inputs as missed wins (`maia_best_prob` + `maia_pre_spread`). Free going forward from the existing reply-spread
  inference; existing rows need a re-inference backfill.
- **Quiet-point material input:** read the material of the played and the best line only at quiet points (the
  next line move is not a capture), so an equal exchange of any length is exactly neutral, and discount the
  settled value by its onset depth. Replaces `decaying_mean`. Needs a recompute of the stored line material.
- **Owner decision (2026-11-21):** pick the input that makes the most sense from first principles; the AUC only
  has to stay in a similar range as the Phase 256 fit (cross-validated 0.902, set 8 0.821). No choice by small
  AUC differences on the 261 owner labels.
- **First step in discuss-phase:** define the quiet-point rule and the onset discount, compute the new input for
  the labelled rows and check the AUC range, before the column and backfill work is planned.
- **Prod runs in sequence:** the re-inference backfill and the material recompute cover about 7.8M `game_flaws`
  rows and must not run beside another operator job (prod `max_connections` is 50).
- **Downstream:** a refit shifts the score distribution, so the facepalm benchmark zones (SEED-261120-bzq) and
  the preliminary bands (`quick/facepalm-prelim-bands`) should be built on, or re-derived from, the new score.
- **Out of scope:** a fit on user blunder-check answers (later, once enough exist; nothing below a score of 0.50
  is served); the `detect_sacrifice` gap for an offered sacrifice the engine declines (noted in the seed).

**Depends on**: Phase 256 (the current model and fit script) and Phase 252's prod line repair (finished, residual
pass 2026-11-18). Independent of Phase 258; built in its own worktree.

### Phase 260: Facepalm as a First-Class Move Quality (SEED-261122-j9p)

**Goal**: Give facepalm blunders (the blunders a player should have avoided at their level) their own slot
wherever move qualities are listed, so a player can go straight to them and compare both sides. Today the
game card's move quality table and the Flaws filter buttons treat every blunder alike. Decisions in the seed
("Decisions locked in the explore session", 2026-11-22).

- **Game card table (`MoveStats`):** an 8th two-sided "Facepalm" row with the ??? icon, a main category like
  the other rows, and its cell cycles through the facepalm plies. A facepalm is a blunder with a facepalm score
  at or above `FACEPALM_HIGH_CUTOFF` (`isFacepalmBlunder`); an unscored blunder is never one.
- **Mobile compact row:** Gem, Great, Best, Mistake, Blunder, Facepalm, chevron. Inaccuracy and Good drop out
  of the compact row; the expanded table keeps all 8 rows.
- **Terminology:** "Blunder" keeps its Lichess meaning (facepalms included) across the app. No blanket copy
  change.
- **Eval chart:** facepalm blunders get a dark red dot, distinct from the regular blunder dot. Reuse the darker
  facepalm tone of the Flaws Timeline band if it fits before adding a theme constant.
- **Flaws filter:** three OR tags, Facepalm, Blunder and Mistake (singular labels, in that order). Blunder means blunders that are not
  facepalms, as in the card table. The Facepalm Score range filter is removed, in the UI and in the API
  (`min_facepalm_pct` / `max_facepalm_pct`). Accepted loss: no filter for very hard-to-see blunders.
- **Blunder row:** in the card table Blunder and Facepalm are disjoint; together they match the Lichess
  blunder count.
- **Desktop card:** the miniboard grows with the 8-row table so both end level.
- **Scope:** frontend and backend. The severity filter of the shared flaw clause builder gains the facepalm
  value; the removal of the range parameters is the last plan and runs after Phase 259 has merged. No
  migration. Decisions in `260-CONTEXT.md`.
- **Parallel with Phase 259:** the cut and its presets in `frontend/src/lib/facepalm.ts` belong to plan 259-12.
  This phase reads the constant and never states the cut as a literal in code, copy or tests, and does not edit
  the cutoff or preset values.

### Phase 261: Blunder-Check Exploration Arm (SEED-261121-t50)

**Goal**: Serve about 20% of blunder windows from a random draw with no facepalm-score cut, record what was
served, and show the score after the answer. The drill only serves blunders at or above `FACEPALM_SCORE_MIN`
(0.70 since Phase 259), so user answers can never validate or refit the score below the cut. The random arm is a
plain sample of what the drill would serve with no cut, usable for any future score without weights. Owner
decisions of 2026-11-21 and the evidence are in the seed.

- **Exploration draw:** one coin flip per blunder slot (share in one named constant, 20%), own and foreign slots
  alike. The draw is the existing game-first probe without the score cut, on scored rows only; among several
  eligible blunders of the picked game it chooses at random, never by score. The main arm keeps the 0.70 cut;
  this phase does not change it.
- **Serve-time record on `blunder_check_windows`:** the arm, the sampling probability and a snapshot of the
  score and its inputs (`loss`, `missed`, `maia_played_prob`, `maia_best_prob`, the best-reply probability). The
  score is a query-time expression that changes with every refit, and a deleted foreign game leaves the window
  with a NULL `game_id`.
- **Counts as normal:** exploration windows give points, streaks, medals and leaderboard score like any other
  window, with no marker the user can see.
- **Avatar feedback in the solution view:** the snapshotted facepalm score, a type line ("The blunder allows a
  material loss / checkmate", "The blunder misses a material win / checkmate") and a "hard to catch" line when
  the snapshotted score is below 50% (a named constant). The type line needs a real material swing of at least 2
  points or a mate in the engine line, walked at serve time with Phase 259's quiet-point rule and stored on the
  window. Shown only when the blunder was caught or missed, never for an unreached blunder (249 D-10). Copy
  stays neutral about whose blunder it is.
- **Calibration script** under `scripts/blunder_check/`: caught rate by score band with intervals, split by arm
  and `is_own`. The analysis itself runs later, on demand; a refit on answers is not part of this phase.
- **First step in discuss-phase:** re-measure on the Phase 259 score. Every number under the seed's Evidence is
  from the old score: pool sizes per user, the caught-rate table and the natural score distribution.
- **Open for discuss and planning:** acceptance criteria (e.g. the served exploration share against the 20%
  target), the prod cost of the probe without the cut and its fit with `ix_game_flaws_facepalm_inputs` (259
  D-17), the served-once rule using up a user's low-score own blunders, and whether the best move's engine line
  is still stored after the Phase 259 drop release (the type line needs it).
- **Out of scope:** the one-tap "unfair" flag (deferred by the owner), review gates or a stop rule for the arm
  (declined by the owner), a fit on user answers, any change to the herring share, the window length or the
  timing.

**Depends on**: Phase 259 (the refit score, the stored inputs and the 0.70 cut; switch and drop releases live on
prod since 2026-11-22). Independent of Phase 260, which is being built in its own worktree at the same time; the
two overlap only where both read the facepalm score.

### Phase 262: Facepalm Promotions as Material and Key-Move Gate (SEED-261122-s0j)

**Goal**: Make the facepalm score see pawn breakthroughs and score every blunder on the move that matches its
type, in one refit. Two changes share the refit and the owner checkpoint. Evidence, the replay numbers and the
example rows are in the seed (refined 2026-11-23); the probe is in the phase directory under `spike/`.

- **Promotions count as material:** a promotion credits the promoting side with the promoted piece minus the
  pawn (+8 for a queen) at the promoting ply, in `app/services/line_material.py`. Adding the gain inside
  `_victim_value` is enough: the quiet walk then continues through a recapture, the first move is covered and a
  line that ends on a promotion is settled by the static exchange. It also fixes the captured promoted queen
  that is credited as 9 today. The 9-ply horizon stays.
- **Desperado test:** the capture of a freshly promoted piece on its promotion square must count. Today's
  desperado exclusion would ignore it once the promotion itself is credited.
- **Key-move gate follows the type rule:** the score reads the opponent's reply only for an allowed loss
  (`loss > 0 and loss >= missed`) and the best move otherwise. Today the gate is `loss > 0`, so any loss above
  zero swaps the best-move term (coefficient 2.08) for the reply term (0.91): 1,728 of 21,147 dev rows (8.2%)
  are typed missed win but scored on the reply, and 40% of them pass 0.7 against 10% of pure missed wins. Both
  twins (Python and SQL) and the type rule then share one condition.
- **Refit and owner checkpoint:** one refit on label sets 1 to 8 with the new inputs and the new gate, the AUC
  as a range check only (259 D-01, D-12), new `FACEPALM_*` constants, and the blocking checkpoint where the
  owner confirms the model and the cuts. At least 13 of the 291 labelled rows have a promotion as the best move
  or the reply (6 labelled facepalm, 7 not). The checkpoint includes a spot-check of about 20 rows that newly
  pass 0.7.
- **Targeted material pass:** only rows with a promotion token in the refutation line, the best line or the
  played move can change (797 of 21,147 on dev, 3.8%), so the prod recompute covers those rows, not all 4.7M.
  The gate change is a constants deploy with no pass (254 D-01). New material values and the new expression
  switch in one release, so users never see half-converted scores (259 D-11).
- **First step in discuss-phase:** re-run the probe on prod lines, with the gate change added, and report how
  many rows enter and leave the 0.7 cut per type and the size of the drill pool. The numbers above are from the
  dev DB and cover the promotion rule only.
- **Open for discuss and planning:** whether the gate needs a minimum loss on top of the type rule (a pawn at
  depth 9 gives a loss of 0.03); whether `LOSS_CAP` 5 is right for a queen promotion (it scores like a mate);
  the covering index `ix_game_flaws_facepalm_inputs` under the new expression; how the snapshots on
  `blunder_check_windows` (Phase 261) and the provisional bands in `app/services/facepalm_zones.py` are treated
  after the refit.
- **Downstream:** run before the benchmark backfill and zones of SEED-261120-bzq, so the benchmark DB is filled
  once with the final rule. The caught-rate calibration of Phase 261 reads snapshotted scores, so answers from
  before and after the switch are on different score versions.
- **Out of scope:** an eval term or a longer horizon for blunders with no material in either line (3,180 dev
  rows, 15%, a known limit); draw blunders such as stalemate or threefold (29 dev rows, 0.14%); a fit on user
  blunder-check answers; the benchmark DB backfill; new label sets.

**Depends on**: Phase 259 (quiet-point rule, stored `material_loss` / `material_missed`, the fit tool) and
Phase 257 (the type rule). Phases 260 and 261 read the score and are live on prod.
