<objective>
Add the facepalm share of the player's blunders over time: per Flaws Timeline point, facepalm
blunders / scored blunders pooled over the same rolling window, computed from `game_flaws` in the
same window walk as the timeline, and shown in its own small chart directly below the Flaws
Timeline for the selected time control.

Purpose: CONTEXT D-03 (pooled share, same x-axis, window and per-TC series), D-04 (untyped),
D-01 (the 0.7 cut).

Output: `fetch_facepalm_share_counts`, the share fields on `FlawTrendPoint` (both stacks),
`FACEPALM_SHARE_MIN_SCORED`, `FacepalmShareChart`, tests.

Recorded assumptions (Claude's discretion, confirmed at UAT):
- Share floor 50 scored blunders per window (orchestrator decision 1, research A1), replacing the
  20 announced in CONTEXT: at 20 the first points of a filling window jump 8 to 19 points on
  benchmark users; a full 100-game window holds about 100 or more scored blunders, so the floor
  only acts while a time control's window fills. The prod probe `temp/facepalm-257/prod_share_probe.sql`
  has not run; it is a manual verification item (plan 07), not a gate.
- The share is shown as a whole percent rounded to nearest; D-07's round-down rule is for the
  facepalm score itself, and the share is a different number. The code says so in a comment at
  the rounding site (Task 2), so a later reader does not "fix" it to a floor.
- The D-03 fallback (a rolling mean of the score) is not built: research found the pooled share
  stable at the chosen floor.
- The share rides on the existing `/flaw-stats` response (CONTEXT discretion), so the dates match
  the timeline point for point and no new endpoint or hook is needed.
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
The scan fired on "fallback" in the roadmap text: "A rolling mean of the score is the fallback if
the share is too jumpy". That names a contingency metric, not a second identity. Primary noun:
the facepalm score of one scored blunder (with its derived type from plan 01); the share is a
pooled ratio over the same rows. Decision: **no-change**. Rationale: no key or identity model
gains a second variant; the fallback metric is not built (research found the share stable at the
chosen floor), and if it were built later it would be another derived series on the same
`FlawTrendPoint`, not a new anchor. No invariant test needed.
</assumption_delta_decision>
